# Intel 13th and 14th gen instability

Part of the parts spec reference: [README.md](README.md) says how it was made and how far to trust each fact. Only a fact given as the maker's own words or figures, with its source and no mark against it, is settled.

## How the pages were read

Researched 2026-11-20.

How I read the pages (read this before trusting a fact):
- Direct downloads of intel.com were blocked by the sandbox proxy (`curl` got "CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403"), so every Intel page went through a fetch tool. That tool turns a page into text, answers questions about it and limits each verbatim quote to about 125 characters. Each time I asked it for exact labels and values. Text in "quotes" below is what the tool returned as verbatim. Where the tool said it had paraphrased, I mark it **(tool paraphrase)**.
- intel.com product pages (the old "ARK") have separate tabs: `/specifications.html`, `/compatible.html` (the "Compatible Products" list) and `/ordering.html`. I read each one separately. Counts of processors in a compatible list are the fetch tool's counts.
- **No Intel chipset spec page I read has a "Memory Types" field.** The fetch tool found no "DDR4", "DDR5" or "Memory Types" text anywhere on the Z790 page or the H610 page when I asked it to look specifically, and it reported the field absent on all the others. So "Intel's spec lists DDR4 and DDR5 for the chipset" is **not** what these pages show. Intel lists DDR4 and DDR5 on the **processor** pages and in support articles. Details in each section.
- "not stated" means it was not in what I read. Anything marked "(from memory, unchecked)" is not from a source. I never opened eBay.

## 13th and 14th gen instability

These are Intel's own words and dates only. I did not use press coverage for any fact here, except one note marked **secondary**.

- **What Intel says it is.** Intel Community post "Intel Core 13th and 14th Gen Desktop Instability Root Cause Update" by Thomas_Hannaford, September 25, 2024 ([ROOT]):
  - "Following extensive investigation of the Intel® Core™ 13th and 14th Gen desktop processor Vmin Shift Instability issue, Intel can now confirm the root cause diagnosis for the issue."
  - Intel "has localized the Vmin Shift Instability issue to a clock tree circuit within the IA core". The circuit is "particularly vulnerable to reliability aging under elevated voltage and temperature."
  - Intel's support article 000099568 describes the effect as a "significant increase to the minimum operating voltage (Vmin) across multiple cores on affected processors", "commonly experienced as consistent application crashes and repeated hangs" ([KB99568]).
- **Via Oxidation, a separate manufacturing issue** ([KB99569]). Each sentence below came back word for word, the same each time, in at least two of four fetches of Intel article 000099569 on 2026-11-20.
  - To the question "The press is reporting a manufacturing issue related to Intel 13th Generation desktop processors." / "What is the relation of that report to this issue?" the answer begins: "The Via Oxidation manufacturing issue and the Vmin Shift Instability issue are separate issues, each with different causes." / "Regarding recent press reports, Intel confirms the Via Oxidation manufacturing issue can affect both Intel Core 13th and" / "14th Gen desktop processors." / "This issue was root caused and addressed with manufacturing improvements and screens in early 2023 on" / "13th Gen desktop processors, these improvements and screens were applied to 14th Generation desktop parts" / "before product launch." / "Intel analysis has determined that only a small number of reports related to the Intel Core 13th and 14th Gen" / "desktop processor Vmin Shift Instability issue can be connected to the manufacturing issue." ([KB99569])
  - To the question "How can I tell the difference between processors affected by Via Oxidation as opposed to elevated voltages?" the answer begins: "There is no practical way to evaluate this on production systems in the field." It goes on: "Intel Core 13th and 14th generation desktop processors affected by the Via Oxidation issue display symptoms similar to" / "processors affected by the elevated voltage related Vmin Shift Instability issue, including OS and application hangs" / "and errors." ([KB99569])
  - **These sentences read oddly together.** Intel says the Via Oxidation issue "can affect both" 13th and 14th gen, and also that the fixes "were applied to 14th Generation desktop parts before product launch". The page does not explain how a 14th gen part could be affected. The quotes are as served, including the comma before "these improvements"; I have not tried to reconcile them ([KB99569]).
- **Which processors, in Intel's words:**
  - Intel article 000102331, "Intel® Core™ 13th and 14th Gen Desktop Processor Vmin Shift Instability Issue – Latest Information", Last Reviewed 07/21/2026: "Intel is aware of reports regarding Vmin Shift Instability affecting some" / "Intel® Core™ 13th and 14th Gen desktop processors and has identified the root cause." ([KB102331])
  - The article says "This article applies to 6 products." These are 13th gen Core i9, i7 and i5, and 14th gen Core i9, i7 and i5. **No i3 family is in that list**, and the article body doesn't mention i3, 65 W or K/KF/KS ([KB102331]).
  - Intel article 000099569: "Not all processors are affected by the Vmin Shift Instability issues." The next sentence is "Intel is investigating options to identify affected processors on end-user systems." ([KB99569])
  - 000099569 introduces its list with "Intel has determined that the following Intel Core 13th and 14th Gen desktop processors could be impacted", but **the list is an image** (alt text: "A table with numbers and letters"), so it can't be read as text. The article names no processor model numbers in its text, and "i3", "65W", "65 W" and "non-K" do not appear in it ([KB99569]).
  - 000099569 asks "Even if my system has never experienced any instability symptoms, should I upgrade the BIOS to one with microcode patch 0x129?" and answers: "Yes, Intel recommends that customers update the BIOS with Microcode 0x129 for systems configured with 13th and 14th" / "Generation Processors with CPUID 0xB0671." It does **not** say that processors with other CPU IDs are unaffected or safe ([KB99569]).
  - Not affected, in Intel's words: "Intel® reaffirms that both Intel® Core™ 13th and 14th Gen mobile processors and future client product families" / "– including the codename Lunar Lake and Arrow Lake families - are unaffected by the Vmin Shift Instability issue." ([ROOT])
  - The 0x125 fix was "to fix the eTVB issue in i9 processors" ([0x129], [KB99568]).
  - Of 0x129, Intel said: "This latest microcode update will primarily improve operating conditions for K/KF/KS processors." ([0x129])
  - On 12th gen, 000099569 answers the question "Why aren’t we seeing this issue on prior-generation unlocked desktop processors?" with: "Based on Intel’s analysis, Intel Core 12th Gen desktop processors are not at risk due to lower voltages and" / "turbo frequencies than 13th and 14th Gen desktop processors." ([KB99569]) This is about processors sold as 12th Gen; it does not cover 13th or 14th gen models (my reading, inference, not stated by Intel). The fetch tool found no "12th", "12th Gen" or "Alder Lake" on the other Intel pages in this section ([ROOT], [KB99568], [KB102331], [0x129], [0x12F], [WARR], [KB99567]), checked 2026-11-20.
  - **Secondary, not Intel's words:** Tom's Hardware (28 July 2024) reported that non-K 65 W-and-up models are affected, citing Intel's statements to The Verge. The fetch tool said the "65W" wording there is the journalist's, not a quoted Intel sentence ([TH]). I found no Intel page that states a 65 W threshold.
- **The four causes Intel named, and the fix for each** ([ROOT]):
  1. "Motherboard power delivery settings exceeding Intel power guidance." Fix: the Intel Default Settings, dated May 2024 in [0x129] and [KB99568].
  2. "eTVB Microcode algorithm which was allowing Intel® Core™ 13th and 14th Gen i9 desktop processors" / "to operate at higher performance states even at high temperatures." Fix: microcode **0x125** (June 2024).
  3. "Microcode SVID algorithm requesting high voltages at a frequency and duration which can cause Vmin shift." Fix: microcode **0x129** (August 2024).
  4. "Microcode and BIOS code requesting elevated core voltages which can cause Vmin shift" / "especially during periods of idle and/or light activity." Fix: microcode **0x12B**.
- **Microcode timeline:**
  - **0x125, June 2024:** "Microcode 0x125 to fix the eTVB issue in i9 processors (June 2024)" ([0x129], [KB99568]).
  - **0x129, post dated August 9, 2024:** "will limit voltage requests above 1.55V". "This patch is being distributed via BIOS update and will not be available through operating system updates." "Users can disable the eTVB setting in their BIOS if they wish to push above the 1.55V threshold." ([0x129])
  - **0x12B, post dated September 25, 2024:** "Intel® is releasing microcode 0x12B, which encompasses 0x125 and 0x129 microcode updates," / "and addresses elevated voltage requests by the processor during idle and/or light activity periods." It "must be loaded via BIOS update", and the rollout "may take several weeks" ([ROOT]).
  - **0x12F, post dated May 1, 2025:** "As part of its effort to continually improve its products, Intel is releasing a new microcode update (0x12F)" / " supplementing the 0x12B microcode update released in September 2024." ([0x12F]). Intel says "This new microcode further improves system conditions that can potentially contribute to Vmin Shift Instability" and "Intel has identified no measurable performance impact with the 0x12F microcode update". It says: "The release of the 0x12F microcode does not alter the root cause determination for the Intel Core 13th and 14th Gen desktop" / "processor Vmin Shift Instability issue" ([0x12F]).
- **What Intel tells users to do now:** "Intel recommends that users adhere to the relevant Intel Default Settings in BIOS" / "and ensure that their systems are running the latest BIOS version containing Microcode 0x12F or later." ([KB102331])
- **Damage that has already happened:** none of the Intel pages I read says in plain words whether a processor that has already become unstable is fixed by the microcode.
  - Intel presents 0x129 as preventive: "The latest microcode update (0x129) will limit voltage requests above 1.55V as a preventative mitigation" / "for processors not experiencing instability symptoms." ([0x129])
  - For chips that already show symptoms, the same post says: "Users experiencing consistent instability symptoms should reach out to their system manufacturer" / "(OEM/System Integrator purchase), Intel Customer Support (boxed processor), or place of purchase (tray processor)" / "further assistance." (There is no "for" before "further assistance" on that page.) ([0x129])
  - Intel article 000099569 asks: "Will the new microcode patch (0x129) for the elevated voltage issue fix my processor if it’s already experiencing issues?" Its whole answer is: "This latest microcode update will primarily improve operating conditions for K/KF/KS processors." / "However, if users are experiencing consistent instability symptoms, they should contact their system manufacturer" / " (OEM/System Integrator purchase), Intel Customer Support (boxed processor), or place of purchase" / " (tray processor) for further assistance." The answer does not say yes or no ([KB99569]).
  - 000099569 also says: "Customers experiencing instability symptoms on 13th and 14th Generation desktop processors should contact their system" / "manufacturer, or in the case of a boxed processor, contact Intel Customer Support." It calls 0x129 "a partial mitigation addressing exposure to elevated voltages" ([KB99569]).
  - (Inference, not stated by Intel: Intel presents 0x129 as a preventive, partial mitigation for chips without symptoms and sends chips with symptoms to the system maker, Intel or the seller. No Intel page I read says any microcode repairs a chip that is already unstable. A skill should not tell a buyer that a BIOS update will cure a used 13th/14th gen chip that already crashes.)
  - Tom's Hardware, 28 July 2024 (Jeff Butts, with Paul Alcorn) ([TH], **secondary**). Its headline, as served on 2026-11-20, is "Intel's CPU instability and crashing issues also impact mainstream 65W and higher 'non-K' models — damage is irreversible, no planned recall". The headline does not say "Intel says"; the words "intel-says-damage-is-irreversible" appear only in the page's URL. The body's only "Intel says" is in the phrase "but Intel says it's much more widespread". The body says, in the journalist's own words and not in quotation marks, "the damage is irreversible, and the chip will need to be replaced". No Intel page I read, and no direct Intel quote in the article, says the damage is irreversible.
- **Warranty:** "Intel has extended warranty coverage by two years for eligible processors affected by the Vmin Shift Instability issue," / "providing coverage for up to five (5) years from the original date of purchase." ([KB102331])
  - KB102331 does not define "eligible" or say what makes a processor eligible. It says nothing about later owners, warranty transfer, proof of purchase, or boxed versus tray ([KB102331]). **Whether a second owner is covered: not stated.**
  - (Inference, not stated by Intel: the cover runs from "the original date of purchase", which a used buyer may not be able to prove.)
  - 000099569 says: "See the Intel Gen 13/14 K-SKU Desktop Processors Warranty Statement for warranty information." ([KB99569]) Intel's warranty statement (article 000099567, "Intel® Gen 13/14 K-SKU Desktop Processors Warranty Statement") says: "Warranty extension applies to new and previously purchased processors," / "if they are one of the Intel Core 13th/14th Gen SKUs listed above." and "This warranty coverage applies to all customers globally." Its list of covered processors did not come through in the fetched text. It does not mention later owners, warranty transfer or proof of purchase ([KB99567]).
  - Intel's earlier forum post (Thomas_Hannaford, August 1, 2024): "We stand behind our products, and in the coming days we will be sharing more details on two-year" / "extended warranty support for our boxed Intel Core 13th and 14th Gen desktop processors." It sent buyers of systems from "OEM/System Integrators" to "your system manufacturer's support team", and buyers of "a boxed CPU" to "Intel Customer Support". The word "tray" does not appear in the post ([WARR]).
